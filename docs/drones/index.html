Who is broadcasting overhead

European and ASTM rules require most drones to broadcast their identity continuously, in the clear, over WiFi and Bluetooth. Two ESP32-S3 boards in the garden listen for those beacons. This page reports how many are audible and how close — and nothing else.

Remote ID beacons carry for kilometres, so most of what a receiver hears is nowhere near it. The large number is not a count of drones overhead — it is the number of distinct airframes whose beacons reached this garden, most of them faintly, from across the city and beyond.

The smaller number is the honest one: how many were loud enough to plausibly be close.

Signal strength is the only distance cue published here, and it is a rough one — a drone behind a building reads weak whether it is near or far. The distribution is the useful part: a long tail at the weak end means the receiver is hearing the whole city, not the garden.

What this page does not publish

A Remote ID beacon is unencrypted and contains a great deal more than a count. Every message the receiver decodes carries:

  • the drone's live position, altitude and height above ground
  • the pilot's position — where the operator is standing
  • the airframe serial number and any registered operator ID
  • the transmitter's MAC address

None of it leaves the house. The station's publisher consumes those messages to maintain a count and a signal-strength histogram, and returns nothing — the raw fields are never republished, never logged to the public feed, and never reach this page. The public topic luisterpost/drones/count contains integers and nothing else.

That is a deliberate limit rather than an oversight. Publishing drone tracks would mean publishing the movements of identifiable people who have no say in it, which is a different thing from publishing the weather.

Counts arrive on the public MQTT broker at wss://live.luisterpost.io, topics luisterpost/drones/count and luisterpost/drones/history. The receiver is two XIAO ESP32-S3 boards watching WiFi and Bluetooth advertisements; its own liveness is on luisterpost/drones/status.

A broadcaster is counted once per five-minute window no matter how many beacons it sends. Airframes that change MAC address between windows may be counted more than once, so the figure is an upper bound on distinct aircraft.
