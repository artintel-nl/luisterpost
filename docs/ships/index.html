The boats on the singel

Vessels broadcast their identity, position and heading on two VHF channels just above the FM band. Most of what this station hears is not shipping at all — it is the small working boats going round and round the canal that rings the old city.

Positions arrive on the public MQTT broker at wss://live.luisterpost.io, topic luisterpost/ships/+/position. AIS is transmitted in clear and aggregated publicly worldwide, so a vessel's position and name are public data in the same way an aircraft's are — and unlike a drone's Remote ID beacon, an AIS message says nothing about where any operator is standing.

There is no marker for the station, and there is no distance on this page. The receiver reports each vessel's range and bearing from itself, and both are discarded before publishing. With an absolute position, a range and a bearing, a single vessel would fix this station exactly — a sharper giveaway than the aircraft feed, where it takes three to triangulate. The station's own position stays where it is published everywhere else, at city precision.

A vessel disappears from the map a few minutes after its last transmission. Small craft report far less often than seagoing ships, so a boat sitting still may go quiet for a while before it fades.
