The station receives on four physical media. Three channels are published live so far — the rest of the instruments report into the station’s own logs.

A luisterpost is what the Dutch military called a signals intercept station — a room full of receivers, pointed at whatever was passing through the air. This one is not on a border or a hilltop. It is an ordinary house in Utrecht, fitted over the years with microphones, antennas, particle detectors and weather instruments, all reporting into one small MQTT broker in the meter cupboard.

Listened to together, the channels describe a city's days better than any one of them could: the blackbird starts before the first plane; lightning arrives on the radio band minutes before the rain arrives on the roof; a Saharan dust plume shows up in the particle counts and, faintly, in the gamma background. This site and its repository publish the code, the hardware notes and — step by step — the data.

Open station

Every channel is being published — firmware, hardware notes, housing builds and data formats. The repository fills up one instrument at a time; data releases follow. Code is MIT, data is CC BY 4.0, and the station location is published at city precision.
