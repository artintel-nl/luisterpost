Detections arrive on the public MQTT broker at wss://live.luisterpost.io. Each one is published live on luisterpost/sound/detection, with the running day on luisterpost/sound/today and the recent feed on luisterpost/sound/recent.

The topic is sound/, not birds/, on purpose. This is an acoustic channel that currently runs a bird classifier; the microphone, the model and the computer behind it are all expected to change, and church-bell identification is likely to join it. So every detection carries a class — "bird" today — and a consumer branches on that rather than on what hardware happens to be in the garden:

{"class":"bird","label":"Great Tit","scientific":"Parus major","confidence":0.94,"ts":"…"}

A bell would arrive as {"class":"bell","label":"Domtoren",…} on the same topic, with scientific simply absent — a bell has no binomial name. Nothing in the public feed names the classifier, the model version or the machine, so replacing any of them breaks nothing downstream.

Confidence is the classifier's own score, not a probability that the bird was there. Common garden species score high because the model has heard a great many of them; a quiet or unusual visitor can be real at a low score, and a passing noise can occasionally score well. The counts are of detections, not of birds — one persistent blue tit at dawn can account for a great many of them.
